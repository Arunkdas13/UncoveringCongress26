Part 1 · Companies and groups that paid the campaigns

Who paid them

Candidates accuse each other of being owned by pharma, tech, oil, Wall Street or the data-center industry. The FEC has the receipts. A company cannot give to a federal candidate from its treasury; it gives through its corporate PAC, funded by its executives and directed by the company. Those checks name the company. Why I built this

In the last weeks before an election, most of what voters see about a congressional race is paid advertising, and most of that money does not come from the candidates. It comes from super PACs, party committees and interest groups whose names appear for a second at the end of a 30-second spot. The filings that say who they are, how much they spent and which ad it bought are all public at the Federal Election Commission. They are just hard to read.

This site puts those filings in one place: every reported dollar spent for or against a 2026 House or Senate candidate, re-added from the original line items, matched to the ads it paid for, and set beside what independent fact-checkers found when they watched those ads. Every number links back to the FEC document it came from, so you never have to take my word for it.

The rules are simple. Nothing here is a forecast. Spending shows where groups are placing bets, not who is going to win. Fact-check ratings are the outlets' own, not mine. And if a total does not reconcile with its line items, it does not get published.
